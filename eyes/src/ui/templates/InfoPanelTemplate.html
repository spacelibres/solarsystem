Your Solar System

Real-time, Real data

Welcome to Eyes on the Solar System! This simulated view of the solar system allows you to explore the planets, their moons, asteroids, comets and the spacecraft exploring them. You can also fast-forward or rewind time, and explore the solar system as it looked from 1950 to 2050. Rewind back to Earth in 1958 to see Explorer 1, skip forward to see the incredible journey of missions like Cassini and Voyager in their entirety, or watch OSIRIS-REx sample the surface of the asteroid Bennu. See the International Space Station's current position over Earth, land on Mars with the Perseverance rover, or watch the Juno spacecraft orbit Jupiter's north pole.

There are hundreds of places to visit and it is all using real data, images, engineering models, and events, just as they happened in space and time.

This app runs in a web browser, and is mobile and tablet-friendly. Most devices with a web browser and an internet connection can enjoy the app, with no download necessary.

Here are some tips to get you navigating around the solar system quickly.

  • Time controls. Use the time controls to move forwards and backwards through space and time. You can simply click or touch the date and time directly and change it to any time you want, or use the rewind, fast-forward and play/pause buttons. For finer control, try the time slider to zip back and forth. If you get lost in time, just click on the “LIVE” button to go to the current time.
  • Moving around objects. Desktop - click/drag, Mobile/Touch - tap/drag. Keyboard: Rotate heading - A/D, rotate pitch - Q/E, rotate bank - Z/C. Add SHIFT for faster speed.
  • Zoom in and out. Desktop - mouse wheel, Mobile/Touch - pinch in/out. Keyboard: Zoom in/out - W/S, Add SHIFT for faster speed.
  • Free-fly mode. Desktop only - press K to enter free-fly mode, and point the mouse where you want to go, and turn using W/A/S/D, and add SHIFT for faster speed. You can fly anywhere in the solar system this way, such as skimming the surface of Mars. Press K again to exit free-fly mode.
  • Click on any label. If you see a label, just click or touch it to instantly travel to the object. Once there, you can see featured events, compare the size to other celestial bodies or spacecraft, change the view, or jump over to other related objects.
  • Menu and search. Just select the magnifying glass and type in what you seek. Alternatively, use the Menu to select any object by name, including planets, moons, spacecraft, asteroids, and comets. On mobile, search and menu are accessed through the "hamburger" menu at the top right.
  • Stories. On the left side of the screen (bottom of the screen on mobile), you will see several stories. Click on one to experience "scrolly-telling". Learn about the Cassini spacecraft discovering geysers on Saturn's moon Enceladus, watch the Perseverance rover land on Mars, or follow the grand tour of the famous Voyager spacecraft.
  • Surface layers. For several planets and moons, you can turn on high quality textures. Just go to the object, then on the panel (left side of the screen for laptops, bottom of the screen for mobile phones) go to the "View Options" tab. On laptops, you will see the "Texture Quality" option, where you select "High Definition." On mobile, you select "View Options" and then "Textures" and you will see the same option. For Earth, Mars, and the Moon, you can also select an even higher-quality texture under the "Surface Map" section, with a description of the source. In the case of Earth, this is a very high-resolution global image mosaic of Earth from the day before!
  • Settings. On desktop and mobile, the show/hide settings button is in the bottom right of the screen. Once opened, you can change the lighting (flood, shadow, and natural lighting), expand to full screen, and turn off and on different layers of visibility (constellations, spacecraft, labels, trails, asteroids, comets, and even the user interface).
  • Telescope mode. When you are at a planet, try telescope mode! Click anywhere on the planet, and you will land at that location. Then turn on constellations and see what's in the sky right now.
